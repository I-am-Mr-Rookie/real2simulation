### Problem statements (themes)

Every project must build toward one of these:

- **Build for Your World.** Bring Meta's models into your domain. Solve a problem you know firsthand, free up time and mental space, and make something possible that wasn't before.
- **Push the Frontier.** Use Meta's models to tackle challenges in software, robotics, manufacturing, energy, materials and beyond. Give a machine a new capability, accelerate a scientific experiment, or unlock a better way to understand and control a physical system. Show what becomes possible when these models meet the real world.

Your project **must incorporate Meta's models**.

> **Note:** Real2Simulation fits *Push the Frontier*: "accelerate a scientific experiment… understand and control a physical system".

## Rules

1. **Open source:** repositories must be public.
2. **Team size:** at most four participants per team. Solo participants are allowed.
3. **Demo requirements:** the demo must only highlight the specific features, code and functionality built during the Hackathon. Judges must be able to clearly identify what was created during the Hackathon. Failure to clearly identify original contributions will result in immediate disqualification, at the Judges' sole discretion.
4. **New work only:** what you present must have been built during the event, and this must be clear in the submission. (Startups may build on existing products or codebases, but must clearly identify what is new.)
5. **Banned projects:** projects are disqualified if they violate legal, ethical or platform policies, or use code, data or assets you do not have the rights to.

### Sample anti-projects

Generic ideas, or ideas that violate platform policies. The list is not exhaustive:

- AI that generates and gives medical advice (e.g. an AI mental-health advisor, a skin-blemish detector)
- Basic RAG applications
- Simple image analyzers
- Simple "AI for Education" chatbots
- AI job-application screeners
- AI nutrition coaches
- Personality analyzers
- Sports analyzers or coaches
- Anything political or involving war

> **Note:** for Real2Simulation, the risky labels are "simple AI for Education chatbot" and "simple image analyzer". Lead with measurement and Real2Sim, and never use sports footage.

## Submission process

- Submit on the **Cerebral Valley platform by Nov 4, 9:00 PM ET** (Nov 5, 8:00 AM Dhaka). **Late submissions are not considered.**
- Teams and startups submit one project; a team appoints one Representative to submit. Solo participants submit their own.
- Double-check that the **repository is publicly accessible**, the **video link is accessible**, and **everyone who worked on the project is listed** on the submission page.
- A **three-minute demo video** is required. It must highlight the features, code and functionality built during the Hackathon. Judges use it as a primary artifact, together with the **code** and the **live product**.

## Judging

**Stage 1 (pass/fail):** the project reasonably fits a theme and reasonably used the Meta Model API and/or Meta's open-weight models, built during the Hackathon.

**Stage 2:** each criterion scored 0–10.

| Criterion | Weight | What judges ask |
|---|---:|---|
| Impact | 30% | What's the real-world potential? Who benefits, and how much does it matter? Could this actually become something people use? Does it fit one of the problem statements? |
| Use of Meta AI tooling | 25% | How creatively did the team use Meta's AI tooling? Did they go beyond a basic integration? Did they surface capabilities that surprised even Meta? |
| Technical depth and execution | 20% | Is the engineering sound and thoughtfully refined? Does it feel thought through with real craft? |
| Demo | 25% | Is it a working, impressive demo? Does the product hold up live? Is the demo video genuinely cool to watch? |

**Stage 3:** the top 5 projects per track go to a final round, where judges watch the video demos live and rank them with the same rubric.

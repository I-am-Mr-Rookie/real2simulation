Why does a ball accelerate downward in a vacuum?

In a vacuum there is no air drag, so the only force on the ball is downward gravity from Earth. That unbalanced force makes its downward velocity grow, which is downward acceleration.

Explain how an Atwood machine changes acceleration when the hanging masses become equal. No calculations needed.

In an Atwood machine acceleration comes from the weight difference between the two sides. As the hanging masses become closer, that net driving force gets smaller so acceleration gets smaller. When they are exactly equal the net force is zero, so acceleration goes to zero and the system stays at rest or moves at constant velocity.

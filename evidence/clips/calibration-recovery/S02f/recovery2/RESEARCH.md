# New terrestrial drop families: precise negatives, not a metric shortlist

## Finding

**Zero new metric-qualified trials.** Two genuinely new NASA source families were acquired lawfully and inspected at consecutive decoded-frame cadence. Both contain real descending motion, but neither establishes the conjunction of independent scale/projection, physical capture clock and a certified untethered fitting interval. This is not evidence that every public source fails. Prior 0/3 remains unchanged; no Gate M or g result.

Full-flight endpoints are NOT required: a suitable partial ballistic interval would suffice. The holds below are clock/geometry/support provenance, not lack of floor contact.

## N01: NASA RVLT air-taxi gantry drop, 2025

- [Source-specific Commons file and PD-USGov-NASA declaration](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:NASA_Drop_Test_Supports_Safer_Air_Taxi_Designs_(LRC-2025-V-06242025-RVLT-Gantry-Drop-Test-With-Slate-V0002050).webm). Credited NASA Langley Research Center. Its history links directly to NASA asset `LRC-2025-V-06242025-RVLT-Gantry-Drop-Test-With-Slate-V0002050`.
- [NASA primary account](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/armstrong/nasa-drop-test-supports-safer-air-taxi-design-and-certification/): approximately 35-foot hoist, forward swing, 10-degree yaw; June 26 test. The catalog ID/file page says June 24. **Date discrepancy retained**, not silently harmonized. Identity is linked by title/description/asset ID, not by assuming dates agree.
- NASA `collection.json` lists the exact mobile and medium asset URLs, saved in `sources/airtaxi-assets.json`. Downloaded via HTTPS, no YouTube. `metadata.json` credits NASA LaRC_OCIO_Video Services_Bono_Herrera. This metadata describes the catalog master, **not** proof of physical camera cadence or a checksum for derivatives.
- Mobile: 320x180, 15 delivered fps, 146.200 s, 6,683,262 bytes. Medium: 1280x720, 30 delivered fps, 146.091 s container / 146.066667 s video, 44,386,565 bytes. The extra medium download was needed to inspect the apparent small overlay/cable geometry; it did **not** reveal a readable independent physical timer. `N01-clock-70s.png` is the retained hypothesis-check image, not evidence that a clock exists.
- Overview at 8-second delivery intervals and 60–84 s search sheet show setup, suspension, multiple angles/replays and impact. Native mobile n1000–1047 shows suspended motion; n1150–1209 spans the later wide-view setup/descent onset; n1200–1247 includes the descending/swinging trajectory and impact. Corresponding medium native n2398–2445 supplies a separate, independently indexed view of that edit. Do not assume `medium n = 2 * mobile n`: durations/cadences differ and no frame correspondence was certified.
- A large gridded calibration backboard is visible. [Littell's 2010 primary technical paper](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20100024230/downloads/20100024230.pdf) documents a 50 x 36 ft array of 24 coded targets, 3-D camera calibration, and 1000 fps for **its historical tests**. This is a concrete geometric lead, not a 2025 calibration certificate. It explicitly places cameras/test article/backboard at different depths. A backboard homography alone is therefore not metric scale at the moving fuselage.
- **HOLD:** no same-test surveyed target coordinates/camera calibration, no physical per-frame clock/edit mapping, no certified last-tether/first-ballistic frame. The 35 ft hoist is not a local scale; the gantry swing is not automatically free fall. Rotation/yaw also makes an arbitrary painted point unsuitable as an assumed centre of mass. Repeated angles represent one test, not several trials.

## N02: Orion structural-test article, April 6, 2021

- [Source-specific Commons file, NASA attribution and PD-USGov-NASA declaration](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:NASA_Drop_Test_of_Orion_Spacecraft_for_Crewed_Artemis_Missionsg.webm). Its history links the NASA image/video-library title. Acquired the **Commons original encoding**, not an unedited camera original. Exact URL/size/SHA-1 and available renditions are in `sources/orion-videoinfo.json`.
- [NASA test account](https://www.nasa.gov/blogs/missions/2021/04/07/orion-makes-a-big-splash-for-artemis-ii/) identifies the April 6 test as second of four in that series, and says 500 sensors. **500 sensors is not 500 fps**. Other Orion-year camera specifications or the 2010 LandIR paper cannot certify this 2021 edited view.
- 1280x720 VP9, 29.554 s, 8,965,877 bytes, reported delivered rate 19001/317. Actual monotonically increasing frame PTS are archived; do not round this metadata to a physical 60 Hz capture clock.
- Overview at 2-second delivery intervals shows suspension, descent and water entry. Native n560–607 is a pre-descent/suspension comparison, not a qualifying fall. Native n660–755 and the larger n660–695 sheet show consecutive descent through water entry. Native-size detail n660 was separately viewed; target plates/support lines are visible, but their surveyed coordinates and support-release boundary remain unverified. Indexing is zero-based decoded display order; window PTS/counts are in `verification.json`.
- **HOLD:** no dimensions tied to the visible target plates or exact test article in this view; no capture/edit timebase map. Capsule rotation/support effects must be resolved before choosing a partial fitting interval. Do not substitute generic Orion dimensions, pool size, guardrail height or assumed terrestrial g for independent calibration.

## Verified event identity

Meta and Cerebral Valley are running a worldwide virtual event, October 26–November 4, with a total cash prize pool of $1 million. Entry is free for selected applicants. [Meta](https://dev.meta.ai/events/global-hackathon), [Cerebral Valley](https://cerebralvalley.ai/e/meta-ai-developer-hackathon).

## Selection, not final judging

The application is evaluated equally on the proposed project's clarity/feasibility/theme fit and demonstrated technical skill in a described AI project. Optional portfolio links support verification. The organizers say Q13–Q16 do not influence selection; those actual prompts were not accessible. Acceptances are rolling, with a 20,000-person cap and possible early closure. Duplicate applications and false information risk disqualification. Final indie judging emphasizes originality, ambition, and a working result. [Meta selection section](https://dev.meta.ai/events/global-hackathon).

**Recommendation:** spend the strongest wording on two things: one feasible, concrete proposal; one technically specific past-project account with honest individual attribution. Avoid a long generic motivation statement, unearned full-stack claims, and feature sprawl. No acceptance probability or unpublished cutoff can be inferred.

For AttemptLens, a single coding-capable Muse Spark model is enough: no image, voice, segmentation, or training stack is needed. Meta describes Muse Spark 1.3 as coding/agent-oriented; its documentation supports schema-constrained responses and tool calling. JSON conformity does **not** establish diagnosis truth. Semantic checks and uncertainty handling remain application responsibilities. Access, event allowances, quotas, billing, and actual integration are unverified. The organizer advertises conditional partner credits, not guaranteed funding for every accepted applicant. Do not buy credits or plan around an allocation before confirmation. [Partner section](https://cerebralvalley.ai/e/meta-ai-developer-hackathon).

## Strategy for Koushik

1. **Solo scope bottleneck:** custom auth, scraping, hosting, and a multi-agent system would consume the sprint. Use one input, one analysis flow, one readable report.
2. **Lead with firsthand relevance:** competitive-programming reflection connects to the existing independent Rookie Coach direction and university context.
3. **Differentiate with evidence:** findings must identify actual attempts/source spans; unsupported diagnosis is withheld. Do not sell a generic chatbot.
4. **Show an evaluation design:** compare against a prompt-only baseline on held-out, synthetic labeled histories; report citation validity and diagnosis quality separately.
5. **Disclose prior work:** Rookie Coach is existing evidence-export work; AttemptLens is a proposed fresh analysis prototype. Reuse only if final rules permit it.
6. **Submit early after review:** rolling admission makes timing material, but never trade accuracy or legal review for speed.

These six points are recommendations, not additional official scoring criteria.
